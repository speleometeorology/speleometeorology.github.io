Speleometeorology

The study of atmospheric processes within caves and subterranean environments.

What is Speleometeorology?

Speleometeorology focuses on the physical processes that govern cave atmospheres, including air exchange with the surface, thermal stratification, condensation corrosion, and the influence of external weather patterns on subterranean systems.

My name is Riannon Colton, and I am a PhD student at the University of Arkansas focused on researching and conducting fieldwork on the measurement and interpretation of cave atmospheric processes. My work emphasizes long-term monitoring of temperature, humidity, airflow, and trace gases in subterranean environments.

This site serves as a repository for ongoing projects, datasets, and technical documentation related to speleometeorology and cave climate research.

Research Areas

  • Cave air temperature and humidity dynamics
  • Seasonal and event-driven airflow patterns
  • Trace gas (CO₂ and Radon) monitoring and cave ventilation
  • Surface–subsurface atmospheric coupling
  • Low-power environmental instrumentation
  • Long-term autonomous data logging
